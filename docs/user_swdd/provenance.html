7 · Provenance

Every fact GL emits remembers where it came from. That record is the deliverable — and it is held to one firm rule: it is documentation, never an input to the proof.

7.1  Recording an origin

Every fire writes an entry into the producing block's exprOriginMap: keyed by the emitted (expression, validity), valued by a list of Origin records. Each Origin names what justified the step. This map is the raw material of every proof chapter and the basis of every auditability claim — what the verifier can re-check depends entirely on what it can read out of the proof graph the prover emits.

There are three failure modes worth distinguishing, because they have different observability:

  • Malformed dependency inside an emitted chapter row. The row claims a citation the verifier cannot reconcile against the rule's shape — the verifier fails the row, and the theorem with it.
  • Missing origin for an emitted derived expression. The chapter walker (§7.3) cannot extend the dependency chain backward; the chapter is truncated or the cited row references something with no provenance entry — again visible, again caught.
  • A theorem or chapter that never enters the verifier's input. The verifier reads the processed proof graph; anything filtered out upstream (or never registered in the global theorem index) is invisible unless a separate registry-to-chapter completeness check exists. This is why the prover side maintains exprOriginMap conservatively: the better protection is to never let a derived fact escape without its origin alongside.

The stored record is two fields — IdOrigin = (tag, dependency-list) — with the fired rule as the first dependency and the matched premises after it. The chapter row decodes that record into the components below.

Decoded row componentHolds
justification (the tag)the tag category — becomes the proof-graph row tag (implication, theorem, equality1, disintegration, …)
rulethe encoded implication key that fired — the first entry in the dependency-list
antecedentsthe matched premises — the remaining dependency-list entries, each an (expression, validity) pair

7.2  Locality

Every origin record is local to the block that produced it. Mail transports provenance downward only — parent → children, via mailOut → mergeBatchInto → child mailIn → child exprOriginMap next cycle. Provenance travels even for statements that themselves never mail: a statement pruned by the equivalence-class canonicalization sweep in the same cycle it registered still ships its origin records before the prune, so a child citing it in a rewrite's history line can always close the chain; likewise a deposit the canonical door rewrote ships the raw spelling's producer record at once, so the canonical form's equality1 bridge resolves at every receiver. There is no upward propagation: a child's records never reach the parent's map. The earlier pendingAncestorOrigins queue (which once wrote a contradiction block's record into every ancestor) was fully removed — no field, no drain remains. The practical consequence: a contradiction's recipe lives only inside its own "__contradiction__" + head block, which the chapter walker enters deliberately when it needs it.

7.3  The chapter walker — four mechanisms

buildStack walks the origin map backward from the proved theorem, emitting one chapter row per visited expression. It is a backtracking walk (false → caller rolls back its snapshots and tries the next candidate; per-key candidates are ordered non-equality tags first, then equality1/equality2). Four mechanisms keep the chapter both truthful and re-checkable:

  • 1 · Ancestor lifting. liftToShallowestOriginAncestor reports each expression at the closest-to-main scope that still has an origin for it. Sound because ancestor facts are universally visible — and lifting moves only toward main, never away (descendant-only facts aren't universally available). The row then truthfully states where the derivation lives.
  • 2 · OR-branch barrier. The lift may not cross a _boundary_orint_ / _boundary_ordis_ delimiter. OR-branch scopes are conditional on a disjunct hypothesis; the OR-family verifier checkers need them kept branch-distinct, or both branches collapse to the same parent cell.
  • 3 · Path-cycle filter. A thread_local path set rejects any candidate whose (lifted) dependencies are already on the recursion path — no chapter row can form a cycle the origin chain termination check would flag. The chapter goal itself is inserted into the path at entry, which rejects the prover's self-applying forward-inference origin (prevents a self-reference leaf).
  • 4 · __contradiction__ fallback. When a negated head has no acyclic direct origin, the walker switches into the matching "__contradiction__" + positive block and resolves it there. This is the only block switch buildStack ever makes; that block is a dedicated child of the LB that proved the contradiction — what is independent is its provenance (no upward propagation), not its tree position.
  • 5 · Cross-chapter admissibility. The export loop keeps a running usage graph of the chapters already written ("chapter of A cites B"). A candidate origin that would cite a proven theorem U while building the chapter of T is accepted only if U's chapter does not — transitively — cite T back; otherwise the candidate is skipped like a path-cyclic one. This is what keeps theorem citations acyclic across chapters, where the path filter (mechanism 3) cannot see.

A hard TRIPWIRE at entry asserts if the hypothetical_disintegration sentinel scope ever reaches the walker — it is a throwaway structural probe and must never appear in a chapter.

7.4  Mechanism 1 — ancestor lifting

A fact (expr, V) has its origin recorded at scope V, but the same fact often carries an independently recorded origin at an ancestor of V as well. Inheritance runs one way: a descendant scope sees every fact its ancestors (and main) installed, but an ancestor does not see a fact installed only in a descendant scope. So the walker cannot simply assume an ancestor proves the fact — liftToShallowestOriginAncestor reports a scope only if that scope has its own recorded origin for the fact. It prefers the shallowest (closest to main) such scope — closer to main means more universally true.

Why lifting is sound. An origin at (expr, V) records that the producing rule fired with every premise available at scope V. Non-root scopes inherit all of main's and every ancestor's facts. If the premises were available at scope V, they were available at every ancestor of V too. So the rule fired soundly at any ancestor — provided the origin chain (the rule's upstream premises) exists at that ancestor. Lifting follows the origin chain upward until it runs out of ancestor-side origin entries.

7.5  Mechanism 2 — the OR-branch barrier

The lift may not cross a _boundary_orint_ or _boundary_ordis_ delimiter. OR-branch scopes are conditional on a disjunct hypothesis; an origin recorded inside a branch is sound only under that hypothesis. Lifting it out of the branch would assert it unconditionally, which it isn't.

7.6  Mechanism 3 — the path-cycle filter

The chapter walker is recursive — it visits an expression, looks up its origin, recurses into each antecedent, looks up their origins, and so on. Without a cycle filter, a chain that loops back on itself (an origin graph with a cycle) would walk forever. The filter is a thread_local set of expressions currently on the recursion path; any candidate whose lifted dependencies overlap the path is rejected.

The path set is chapter-local. A previously proven theorem used as a rule enters the chapter as a foundation leaf (a theorem row with no dependencies) — the walk does not continue into that theorem's own chapter. Two chapters can therefore each ground themselves in the other's theorem while every per-chapter check passes: within each chapter the cited theorem is bedrock, yet globally the pair justifies only itself. Two layers close this gap. The exporter prevents it: mechanism 5 (§7.3) consults the usage graph of already-written chapters and rejects any theorem citation that would close a cross-chapter cycle, falling back to an alternative origin exactly as this filter does. The verifier independently proves it: the global theorem usage termination check builds the directed graph "the chapter of theorem A cites theorem B" over all proven theorems and fails every theorem that lies on a cycle. Per-chapter acyclicity (this filter) plus acyclicity of that theorem-usage graph give whole-graph acyclicity, because chapters connect to each other only through theorem citations.

7.7  Mechanism 4 — the __contradiction__ fallback

When a chapter row's expression has no acyclic direct origin in the current LB, the walker switches into the dedicated child LB whose exprKey is "__contradiction__" + the expression's negation (either polarity: a negated row leads to the positive-seed block, a positive row to the complement block that assumed its negation) — located by walking the ancestor chain with findChild — and resolves the chain there. That block is a true child of the LB that proved the contradiction (its parentMemory points back at it); what is independent is the provenance (no upward propagation), not the tree position. This is the only block switch buildStack ever performs; every other walk stays inside one LB's origin map.

The TRIPWIRE. The sentinel scope hypothetical_disintegration is a throwaway used by structural probing inside the prover. It must never appear in a chapter row — if buildStack ever encounters it on the recursion path, it asserts at the function's entry. The assert is load-bearing: a sentinel leaking into the chapter would silently corrupt the verifier's scope walk.

7.8  Provenance is never a proof input

This is the single firmest rule about the origin map, worth stating plainly.

The proving logic does not branch on the origin map. No rule fires, no admission is granted, no branch is taken because of what is — or isn't — in exprOriginMap: no proof decision turns on the map's contents. One redundant dedup gate inside applyEquivalenceClass still reads the map, but it duplicates a statement-level check, so no theorem depends on it — and it is slated for removal. This is what makes the forthcoming origin-map-free silicon build possible as a design target — the same theorems are intended to prove without the map present — though the silicon path is a design consequence of this contract, not a currently-implemented check. Provenance is an output that rides alongside the proof, not a wheel it turns on.

The flip side is an obligation: precisely because nothing re-derives it, every state change that alters a derived fact must emit the matching origin line, or the audit graph silently rots. Concretely: when an equivalence-class rewrite touches an expression that already has a history line and is not a goal in intToBeProved, it must emit a fresh equality1 line for the rewritten form (goals are exempt — they have no upstream line to extend). “Write it, never branch on it, always keep it complete” is the whole contract — and it is what lets an independent verifier re-check every theorem that reaches its processed proof-graph input, plus the auxiliary files it explicitly consumes, without trusting the prover at all (the equality1 row at the end of the §4.2.5 Gauss trace is exactly such a re-checked line). Anything filtered upstream of that input is outside the verifier's sight by construction — see §7.1 for the three visibility cases.

7.9  Independent Lean certificate

The processed Peano, Gauss, and FTA shortcut proof graphs can be translated into typed neutral certificates and checked by Lean 4. This path is downstream of GL: it reads an existing graph and does not run the prover. The certificate retains theorem, chapter, row, dependency, action, source-hash, and exclusion records; the Lean renderer emits one named fact and one manifest disposition for every selected row.

The exported GL theory uses ordinary types. There is one abstract carrier α. A set is a predicate α → Prop; binary and ternary operations are relations over α. No GL value becomes a type and no GL term indexes a type family. Lean's kernel has its own dependent foundation, but the object theory presented to it stays in this ordinary predicate fragment.
Every row is checked by an explicit proof term built from its own citation, not by search over everything above it. A rule firing is replayed literally: the row applies the rule GL cited to the premises GL cited, in the rule's own premise order. The 136 Gauss and FTA integration-goal definition expansions introduce their outer parameters explicitly and close the definitionally equal unfolded sides with Iff.rfl; no generated line ends in an unrestricted simp tactic. Equality substitution eliminates the named equalities in order, witness projection follows the named conjunction path, and scoped facts are specialized at their recorded prefixes. The disequality-symmetry action applies equality symmetry to the proposed reverse equality and passes that result directly to the one cited disequality. A scoped task-formulation row returns its own introduced scope premise directly. An equality2 row chains its two cited equalities with equality transitivity in certificate order. A contradiction row applies the cited negation to its cited positive complement; when that negation belongs to a recorded contradiction scope, the row first introduces exactly that scope premise. During a premise-element action, the cited implication is the recorded scope template and the row returns the unique matching introduced premise. An implication action applies its cited rule and supplies the cited premises in that rule's order; scoped citations are first specialized to the recorded scope, including the matching component of any shared witness bundle. The difference between an empty-bound integration reformulation and its cited definitional equivalence is also replayed directly: the selected output and its two premises refute the universal counterexample. A vacuous-truth row likewise eliminates the unique complementary pair in its own citation directly. Bounded integration reformulations use the same explicit counterexample refutation after entering any recorded scope. An OR-disintegration row enters its licensed branch and returns that branch assumption directly, making the exported identity implication visible. A constructed-OR theorem now shows its case split and left-associated disjunction constructors directly, ending with the cited parent that proves the final alternative from the preceding negations. OR convergence similarly splits the cited compact OR and applies the matching branch result; compact-existence branches visibly unpack their witness before that application. Theorem-reformulation rows likewise show the constructed set or relation and apply their single cited source theorem directly to it and the recorded premise. The FTA theorem-level OR elimination derives the OR parent's domain premise from the multiplicative preorder witness, then visibly splits the compact OR and applies the two branch interfaces. Equality-substitution rows similarly name and eliminate every cited equality in order, then return the rewritten cited source; no unrelated equality is searched. Disintegration rows show their exact conjunction path, shared decoded existence witness, or classical implication from a cited negated pair, including inside recorded scopes. The renderer and all three generated proof modules contain no grind. The difference matters: before explicit replay, a passing build showed only that each row followed from something already derived. It now shows that GL's recorded justification is the one that works, and a wrong or incomplete citation breaks the build instead of passing quietly.

Restricting the context immediately found one such row. Its two cited premises were existence facts that GL states about a single witness inside one proof scope, while the translation had been quantifying each row's witness separately — so the row had been closing through a route GL never recorded. A scope's witness is now introduced once and shared by every row that speaks about it, which is what makes that row provable from its own citation.

The current full Peano selection contains all 64 theorems in the live graph, 138 chapters, and 3,107 source rows with an empty exclusion list — possible since the inter-chapter theorem-usage cycle was eliminated from the proof graph (mechanism 5 in §7.3 prevents it at export; the theorem usage termination check proves it). The historical 65-theorem selection, which excluded the former two-theorem cycle, remains tracked as a record. The dependent Gauss certificate pins the full Peano certificate by hash and adds all 29 Gauss main theorems, 33 chapters, and 1,511 rows. Together those two base modules check 93 public theorems and 4,618 row facts. Induction is equally visible: each public theorem accepts the relational induction principle as an explicit proposition premise instead of importing it through an axiom. The generated typing and zero helpers apply their named rules, the successor case returns its named assumption, induction-hypothesis steps apply that hypothesis to their introduced premises, and the public theorem applies the resulting induction property explicitly. Generated source containing an admission shortcut or grind is rejected before the Lean build.

The FTA shortlist adds a separate generated scope of 79 theorems, 109 chapters, and 2,835 rows, needing no internal support theorems. Its certificate pins 19 Peano and two Gauss theorems as external facts. Four Peano citations differ only by universal-premise order or by renamed compiled definitions; the certificate checks the permutation, variable bijection, and exact definition bodies before accepting each adaptation. Each FTA theorem then receives its required complete, universally quantified target theorems as explicit Lean proposition parameters. Each row specializes them at its current FTA anchor. It never calls the live generated Peano or Gauss theorem proofs.

FTA-local definitions live under GLExport.FTA. The exporter creates only a definition delta, the FTA theorem module, and its manifest, so the reviewed Peano and Gauss files remain byte-identical. FTA passes its certificate/renderer tests and separate kernel compile through the 19 pinned Peano and two pinned Gauss theorem propositions; the Gauss module import supplies shared definitions only, while internal FTA theorem calls forward the explicit propositions required by their dependency closure. FTA does not consume Peano or Gauss proof rows. Across all three exports the renderer emits and kernel-checks 172 public theorems, 280 chapters, and 7,453 row facts. The default pinned Lean target contains the live 93-theorem Peano/Gauss base and its 4,618 row facts; FTA stays in a separately compiled interface module.

A direct compile of the separate FTA module passes. Its induction premise is an explicit schema over an arbitrary FTA anchor, and its external parameters are complete theorem propositions. A zero-arity definition compaction can therefore introduce the proposition's arbitrary context and replay the cited earlier FTA theorem there. No Peano or Gauss proof content is imported.

Gauss integration scopes are represented by ordinary universal quantifiers. For the eight reformulated existence theorems, the renderer constructs the defined set or relation as a predicate lambda, proves its defining condition, and applies the cited GL theorem to that concrete witness. This is the GL well-defined-output contract made constructive; it adds neither an existence axiom nor a dependent GL type family.

The official release regenerates all three exports in dependency order, runs the certificate/renderer suite for their declared interfaces and the pinned Peano/Gauss Lean build, and then copies the generated certificates, modules, project files, and manifests byte-for-byte. The private recovery snapshot copies the same current results but never invokes the exporter. The host-local .lake/ build cache belongs to neither package.

That closes the prover subsystem: the block it runs in, the engine that fires, the compilation that makes it canonical, the mechanisms that reason, the parallelism that scales it, and the provenance that makes all of it checkable.
