10 · Reformulation & head switch

A single proof can yield several theorems if you read it from several angles. This chapter is GL's machinery for that — the head-switch walk that produces a theorem's contrapositive, the way the reverse direction of a theorem is now proved for real, the contradiction-theorem reconstruction that lifts a self-refuting branch into a parent-scope claim, the OR-theorem assembly that fuses a mutually-exclusive pair into a single disjunction, and the difference between sound (main) and unsound (incubator) reformulation.

10.1  Why reformulation

GL only reasons forward: from premises through hash-rule fires to a head. But a forward proof of A → B is, logically, also a proof of ¬B → ¬A — the contrapositive. Likewise, an OR branch that self-refutes proves the negation of its assumption, which fed back to the parent looks like a positive claim. And a pair of mutually-exclusive implications (“if not A then B”, “if not B then A”) together constitute a disjunctive theorem (“A ∨ B”).

Each of these is a different way of reading the same proof — they do not require additional proving work. Reformulation is the umbrella name for the mechanisms that read the work the prover has already done and emit the additional theorems it implies.

10.2  The head-switch walk — headSwitchOne

The head-switch walk is the simplest possible thing it could be: a pure structural string rewrite that takes a chain P₁ ∧ P₂ ∧ … ∧ P_k ∧ !Q → R and produces P₁ ∧ P₂ ∧ … ∧ P_k ∧ !R → Q. The “head” (the implication's conclusion) is swapped with the right-most negated premise. No proof work, no search — just rebuild by concatenation.

The “empty inner bound list” gate. Head switch only fires when the target !Q premise has no inner >[…] binding its own variables. Crossing a binder would require alpha-renaming and bound-set juggling — which is exactly what the prover does dynamically anyway, but the head-switch helper is deliberately the cheap rewrite, not a re-prover. Cases that need binders are picked up by the regular proof loop via back-reformulation (§10.5).

10.3  Two call sites — pre-emit and post-prove

headSwitchOne is invoked from two places, both unconditional on a qualifying theorem shape. Each yields a different artefact stream.

SiteWhenProduces
Pre-emit passJust before the first compileAndRegister in analyzeExpressionsAdds the contrapositive of every qualifying conjecture to the goal pool, so the single prove pass reaches both readings at once. Replaces the deleted multi-iteration loop.
Post-prove walkImmediately after prove() returns, before compressor + OR-theorem constructionPopulates orPairsFromHeadSwitch: every (!A → B, !B → A) pair the prover happened to derive becomes a candidate for OR-theorem assembly (§10.7).

10.4  Both directions, proved for real

Many theorems have a natural reverse direction — the same relation read with the output role moved from the head to a premise (for an operator a∘b=c: "given a,b find c" versus "given c,a find b"). GL used to fabricate that reverse direction after proving the forward one and stamp it method = mirrored statement — a row asserting a theorem the prover never derived. Because the swap is the converse of a functional relation and not a logical equivalence, that row could be quietly wrong.

GL no longer does this. The conjecturer folds each conjecture's reverse-direction mirror into the real prove pool (conjectures.txt), so it runs through the same counterexample filter and the same proof engine as every other conjecture. A true reverse direction is proved for real — it appears with method = direct or induction and carries its own proof chapter; a false one simply fails to prove and never appears. The proof is now explicit in both directions.

No unproven rows. There is no longer a mirrored statement method or mirrored from tag, and the verifier no longer carries a mirror checker. Every theorem in the global list is backed by a real proof chapter. (The distinct, sound equality mirror (=[a,b]) → (=[b,a]) and the head-switch contrapositive of §10.2 are unaffected — both were always genuine.)

10.5  Back-reformulation — the incubator-only path

Back-reformulation is a stricter cousin of head-switch that operates deep inside the prover, on partially-built intermediate states rather than on top-level theorems. The hot example: when an incubator block has proved a chain part-way but not its head, the back-reformulation pass tries to recast the chain so a different intermediate becomes the head. If the recast lands on a known derivable fact, the chain closes.

Back-reformulation is gated by !parameters.ban_disintegration and additionally incubator_mode: it does not run in main batches. The reason is that back-reformulation, by construction, weakens some preconditions — useful for the incubator's heuristic fact harvest, unsound for a main proof.

main: reformulated from

  • Tag in the proof graph for a row whose head was reached via a head-switch path the verifier can re-check.
  • Sound — mathematics is preserved.
  • Verifier checker: _check_reformulation against a real GL binary.

incubator: incubator back reformulation

  • Tag for an incubator-only reformulation step that the verifier does not re-check by content.
  • Unsound in general — the incubator deliberately weakens to harvest facts.
  • Verifier just counts; the binary "Incubator" resolves to None and the structural checker bypasses.
Do not group them. A common reading error is to treat reformulated from and incubator back reformulation as the same tag with different labels. They are structurally different: the main-side row is a verifier check; the incubator-side row is a counter. Mixing the two — for example, accepting an incubator back-reformulation as discharge for a main goal — silently widens the main proof's accepted-input set.

10.6  Contradiction-theorem reconstruction

An OR branch (§4.4) is opened with a disjunct asserted as a local assumption. If the branch's other facts contradict that assumption — both X and !X become derivable inside the branch — the branch is self-refuting. Mathematically, this proves ¬(assumption) in the parent scope. The prover reconstructs this as a theorem and discharges it upward.

10.6.1  What primedForContradiction does

A branch that proves a contradiction satisfies the normal “my goal is done” criterion: the contradiction is the goal in the negated head sense. Without intervention, deactivateRecursively would tear the branch down immediately — but the parent still needs the branch alive for one more cycle to harvest the reconstructed theorem. primedForContradiction is a per-LB flag that suspends deactivation until the discharge has fired. After discharge it is cleared and the normal lifecycle resumes.

10.7  OR-theorem assembly

A theorem whose head is an OR is not proved by the prover directly — proving a disjunction by forward inference would require choosing one of the disjuncts, which is the case-analysis the prover deliberately avoids. The trick is to look for a mutually-exclusive pair of proved theorems: !A → B and !B → A. Together, those two readings say “at least one of A, B holds” — which is exactly A ∨ B.

10.7.1  The pair-canonicalisation rule

The pair (¬A → B, ¬B → A) is symmetric: it could equally well be written (¬B → A, ¬A → B). To avoid emitting two OR theorems for the same logical pair, the disjunct set is canonicalised by sorting: (min, max) by the disjunct expressions' string form. Once canonicalised, each unordered pair maps to exactly one OR theorem.

10.8  The five method tags

Every entry in global_theorem_list.txt carries a method tag that names how the theorem was obtained. The five values are exclusive and cover every emission path.

TagSourceVerifier checker
directThe literal forward proof — the head was reached by the regular hash-engine fire.theorem goal reached (resolves to the chapter's last row).
inductionThe induction triad fired: base case, step case, and typing all landed.check_induction_condition + check_zero + induction_typing across the triad chapters.
reformulated statementA head-switch-reachable form whose proof was found via reformulation rather than direct fire — the row form changed structurally._check_reformulation against the real GL binary.
incubator back reformulationAn incubator-only reformulation step (§10.5); the incubator deliberately weakens preconditions to harvest facts, so it is unsound in general.check_incubator_back_reformulation — the witness-elimination rewrite is now checked structurally by content: the direct form must be the cited existence source with its witness eliminated through the trailing equality. (The check certifies the rewrite step, not the harvested fact's global soundness.)
or theoremAn OR head assembled from a mutex pair (§10.7).or theorem check — the verifier validates the parents and the disjunct canonicalisation.
Method labels can shift across commits without proof loss. The same theorem (same raw MPL content in theorems.txt) can be emitted under different method tags depending on which path closed first. theorems.txt is the authoritative regression target; global_theorem_list.txt's method-count deltas are not direct loss signals.

10.9  Failure modes

“The mirrored from count went to zero — did we lose theorems?”
No. The mirrored statement method was removed; each reverse direction that used to be fabricated is now proved for real and re-emitted under direct or induction. The raw set in theorems.txt is the source of truth — a genuinely missing theorem is one whose raw MPL is absent there. Check: grep -F the raw content in theorems.txt on both sides.
“OR theorem missing the parents — chapter walker can't find them”
The parents are moved to compressed_out_theorems.txt by design (D-55 dedup). The verifier knows to look there. Check: the parents exist in compressed_out_theorems.txt; the OR theorem's chapter cites them with the right validity.
“Verifier reports reformulated from failure 1”
The cited binary doesn't resolve. Often: a row was tagged reformulated from on an incubator batch by mistake (incubator uses incubator back reformulation instead). Check: the chapter's anchor; if it's an incubator anchor (AnchorIncubator8 / AnchorIncubator3), the row's tag should be the incubator one. See [V-6 / V-7 in the rung-1 verifier report].
“Contradiction discharge fires but parent shows no negated fact”
The branch LB was deactivated before discharge had a chance to fire — primedForContradiction was not set. Check: the OR branch construction sets the flag at branch open; deactivation respects it.
“OR theorem assembled from a pair where one was head-switched and the other was not — they look mutex but aren't”
The pair has to come from orPairsFromHeadSwitch, populated by the post-prove pass. A pair from any other source has not been validated as mutex. Check: every OR-theorem construction reads from orPairsFromHeadSwitch; no ad-hoc pairing.
